# Customer Pilot Playbook

Updated: 2026-05-17

This playbook prepares 3C Simulator for a controlled paid pilot. It is not a production SaaS launch plan.

## Positioning

3C Simulator is sold in the pilot as a decision-support workspace for campaign, competitor, and crisis planning. It helps teams pressure-test ideas before real spend and produces evidence packs for leadership or client conversations.

Outputs are scenario-planning estimates, not guaranteed predictions of market behavior, revenue, ROI, or crisis outcomes.

## Readiness

| Path | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Local demo | Ready |
| Controlled private pilot | Conditional candidate |
| Public pilot | Blocked |
| Public internet exposure | Blocked |
| Production customer deployment | Blocked |

## Pilot Offer

The commercial pilot should be sold as a guided engagement, not self-serve SaaS:

- 2-4 week controlled pilot.
- 1-3 approved campaign or crisis scenarios.
- No PII, raw CRM records, or live integrations.
- Guided onboarding and weekly review calls.
- Final strategy pack with source/provenance labels and limitations.

## Entry Criteria

Start a customer pilot only when:

- The customer accepts that outputs are estimates for decision support.
- The customer can provide anonymized or approved campaign briefs.
- No regulated personal data, raw CRM exports, API keys, customer lists, or confidential live social data are required.
- Source/provenance labels remain visible in demos, screenshots, exports, and reports.
- The deployment owner confirms manual credential rotation and environment-specific secrets before any hosted pilot.

## Recommended Pilot Flow

1. Discovery call: confirm use case, decision owner, risk tolerance, data boundaries, and success criteria.
2. Data intake: collect only approved campaign briefs and non-sensitive context.
3. Demo calibration: show Demo Mode first so source labels and limitations are understood.
4. Scenario setup: define campaign, audience, channels, competitor context, and risk notes.
5. Simulation review: review KPIs, assumptions, segment response, risk drivers, and action plan.
6. Strategy pack: export Brand Executive Summary or Agency Client Pitch Summary.
7. Decision workshop: decide launch, revise, validate further, or hold.
8. Exit review: document value, gaps, buyer objections, and next commercial step.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Owns business decision and pilot budget. |
| Pilot lead | Coordinates scenario intake, review sessions, and feedback. |
| Data approver | Confirms all input data is allowed for pilot use. |
| Operator | Runs the local/controlled environment and keeps source labels visible. |
| Reviewer | Evaluates outputs against business usefulness and trust criteria. |

## What To Avoid

- Do not sell the pilot as production SaaS.
- Do not claim exact ROI, exact conversion lift, or guaranteed market outcomes.
- Do not import raw CRM records, customer lists, PII, or live social-listening feeds.
- Do not remove Demo Mode, Local Estimate, Live Backend, or Backend Verified labels.
- Do not present Local Estimate or Demo Mode as real observed market evidence.

## Deliverables

- Approved scenario list.
- Brief Quality Score and missing-field recommendations.
- Scenario dashboard with source/provenance labels.
- War Room or competitor/crisis scenario summary when relevant.
- Action Plan with creative, channel, crisis-prevention, and validation recommendations.
- Strategy Pack PPTX.
- Pilot exit checklist and go/no-go recommendation.
