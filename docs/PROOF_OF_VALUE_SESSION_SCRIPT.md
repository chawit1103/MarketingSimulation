# Proof-of-Value Session Script

Updated: 2026-05-17

Use this script for a customer-facing proof-of-value session based on one approved campaign brief or approved historical campaign scenario. The session should show value from the customer's approved business context without using PII, raw CRM records, customer lists, secrets, or live integrations.

## Pre-Session Requirements

- [ ] Customer has completed [Customer Brief Intake Template](CUSTOMER_BRIEF_INTAKE_TEMPLATE.md).
- [ ] Customer has explicitly approved the brief or historical campaign scenario for proof-of-value use.
- [ ] Customer has confirmed the intake includes no PII, raw CRM records, customer lists, live integration credentials, API keys, provider tokens, passwords, graph credentials, or other secrets.
- [ ] Operator has confirmed source/provenance labels are visible in the relevant flows.
- [ ] Operator has selected whether the session uses local demo, controlled backend, or a hosted controlled pilot environment.
- [ ] Operator has prepared the expected deliverables from [Proof-of-Value Deliverables](PROOF_OF_VALUE_DELIVERABLES.md).
- [ ] Operator has confirmed no production-readiness, public-pilot, guaranteed-prediction, or exact ROI/ROAS claims will be made.

## 30-Minute Session Flow

| Time | Activity | Talk track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-5 min | Confirm scope and data boundary | "Today we are using one approved campaign brief for decision support. We are not using PII, raw CRM data, customer lists, live integrations, or secrets." |
| 5-10 min | Brief Quality Score | Review whether the campaign brief has enough objective, segment, channel, competitor, risk, and KPI context. |
| 10-15 min | Campaign Simulation Summary | Review directional segment reactions and where the brief may create friction or opportunity. |
| 15-20 min | A/B/C Creative Comparator | Compare creative directions and discuss which tradeoffs matter most to the customer. |
| 20-24 min | Risk and Crisis Watchouts | Review sensitive claims, competitive risks, cultural concerns, and monitoring needs. |
| 24-27 min | Revised Brief v2 | Show how the brief can be tightened before launch or further testing. |
| 27-30 min | Close and next step | Agree whether a controlled paid pilot is worth scoping, including validation goal, data boundary, and retention/deletion ownership. |

## 60-Minute Workshop Flow

| Time | Activity | Talk track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0-5 min | Introductions and consent | Confirm the approved brief, pilot-use consent, and no-disallowed-data boundary. |
| 5-12 min | Business context | Ask the customer to explain the campaign objective, audience, channel plan, competitors, and risk concerns. |
| 12-20 min | Brief Quality Score | Review strengths, gaps, and assumptions in the brief. |
| 20-30 min | Campaign Simulation Summary | Review segment reactions, friction points, and decision implications. |
| 30-38 min | A/B/C Creative Comparator | Compare creative options and identify the most useful next validation question. |
| 38-45 min | Risk and Crisis Watchouts | Review message risks, escalation triggers, and monitoring priorities. |
| 45-52 min | Revised Brief v2 | Co-review revised positioning, clearer KPIs, and recommended campaign adjustments. |
| 52-56 min | Strategy Pack PPTX | Walk through the executive-ready output and confirm provenance and limitations are visible. |
| 56-60 min | Paid pilot close | Define the pilot question, approved data inputs, success criteria, deployment requirements, and next meeting owner. |

## Live Walkthrough Sequence

1. Open the approved campaign context.
2. Confirm source/provenance label visibility.
3. Show Brief Quality Score.
4. Show Campaign Simulation Summary.
5. Show A/B/C Creative Comparator.
6. Show Risk and Crisis Watchouts.
7. Show Revised Brief v2.
8. Show Strategy Pack PPTX structure or generated export.
9. If aggregate historical actuals are approved, show optional Actual vs Estimate comparison.
10. End with limitations, recommended validation, and controlled paid pilot next step.

## Questions To Ask The Customer

- Which decision would this help you make before spending campaign budget?
- Which recommendation feels most useful, and which feels least credible?
- Which audience segment or risk signal should be validated first?
- Which creative direction would you want to test or revise?
- Which aggregate actuals could be approved for a later Actual vs Estimate comparison?
- What data must remain out of scope for a controlled paid pilot?
- Who would approve pilot scope, retention/deletion rules, and artifact sharing?

## Closing Call-To-Action

If the customer sees enough value to continue, propose a controlled paid pilot scoping step:

- one approved campaign or historical scenario,
- one or two validation questions,
- no PII, raw CRM records, customer lists, secrets, or live integrations,
- agreed source/provenance labeling,
- agreed deliverables and review dates,
- agreed retention/deletion owner,
- agreed deployment and access controls if hosted.

Position the next step as a controlled paid pilot candidate, not production SaaS, public pilot access, public internet exposure, or guaranteed prediction.
