# Proof-of-Value Outreach Message Pack

Updated: 2026-05-17

Use these customer-facing messages to introduce, book, and follow up on the 3C Simulator Proof-of-Value Workshop. The workshop uses one approved campaign brief or approved historical campaign scenario to create decision-support outputs and assess whether a controlled private pilot is worth scoping.

Keep the boundary clear in every message: no PII, no raw CRM records, no customer lists, no secrets, no live integrations, no production deployment, no guaranteed prediction, no exact ROI/ROAS, and no replacement for market research.

## Objection Response: "We Do Not Want Another AI Demo"

That makes sense. This is not positioned as a generic AI demo.

The workshop uses one approved campaign brief and produces a concrete proof-of-value package your team can critique: brief quality, creative comparison, risk watchouts, revised brief, Strategy Pack outline, and next validation step.

If the output is not useful for a real campaign decision, we stop there. If it is useful, we can discuss a controlled paid pilot.

## Objection Response: "We Cannot Share Customer Data"

You do not need to share customer data.

The workshop does not require PII, raw CRM records, customer lists, contact details, account-level records, secrets, or live integrations. We can use an approved campaign brief, anonymized campaign code, broad budget band, and aggregate-only actuals if those are approved.

If that boundary still feels too sensitive, we can start with a synthetic or anonymized scenario.

## Objection Response: "Can This Predict ROI?"

No. The workshop is not positioned as exact ROI/ROAS forecasting.

It provides a decision-support estimate: where the brief is strong or weak, how creative routes compare, what risks to watch, and what should be validated next. If aggregate actuals are approved, any comparison is directional and diagnostic.

## Objection Response: "We Already Have Market Research"

That is useful, and this does not replace it.

The Proof-of-Value Workshop can sit before or alongside research by helping the team sharpen the brief, compare creative routes, identify risks, and define the next validation question. Existing research can inform the brief if it is approved to share and does not include disallowed data.
